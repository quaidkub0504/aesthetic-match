The patient experience

Five steps from a feeling to a booked consultation.

A visitor who only knows that something bothers them is guided to a qualified provider — without ever needing the name of a treatment.

Step 01

Identify concerns

They select goals — fine lines, pores and texture, pigmentation, volume, laxity, acne scars — instead of searching for "Botox near me."

Step 02

Build a profile

Budget range, downtime tolerance, prior experience, and travel radius — plus whether they prefer subtle or more noticeable results.

Step 03

Discover providers

Relevant local providers with verified credentials, applicable services, consultation fees, and authentic reviews.

Step 04

Request a consult

The patient requests a visit. The clinician — not the platform — determines medical appropriateness.

Step 05

Continue the journey

After the clinician's assessment, an optional personalized plan supports education, follow-up, and rebooking.

How this could differ from existing software

Not another booking app. Three deliberate differentiators.

Comparable capabilities exist across aesthetic booking, marketing, and imaging tools. The proposed edge is this particular combination — and the discipline behind it.

Concern-first discovery

A patient describes what bothers them rather than searching for a specific procedure. The platform translates goals into relevant consultations.

Suitability, not popularity

Credentials, relevant services, patient preferences, geographic proximity, and consultation availability influence matches.

Paid placement is disclosed, never disguised as clinical fit

Closed-loop measurement

Track the journey from first questionnaire to booked consultation, attended appointment, and completed treatment.

Only where privacy and consent permit

Development roadmap

Key risks & safeguards

Built with the hard parts named up front.

A concept is only as credible as its honesty about what could go wrong — and how it's handled.

Two-sided marketplace

Providers want demand; patients want choice. Start with a practice-branded tool that delivers value without needing marketplace scale.

Privacy

Determine whether HIPAA, FTC health-app rules, state privacy law, and BAAs apply to specific data flows. Minimize collection; protect data in transit and at rest.

Clinical boundaries

Never present matches as diagnoses, guarantee outcomes, or auto-recommend invasive procedures without appropriate clinical oversight.

Provider verification

Validate licensure and credentials, and keep records current. The "verified" badge is only as good as the process behind it.

Referral & advertising rules

Review fee-splitting, patient inducements, professional advertising, and sponsored-ranking disclosure before launch.

Demand generation

The platform doesn't create traffic by itself. Budget for SEO, local partnerships, and marketing — and measure acquired patients, not clicks.

Recommended next steps

Prove the journey before building the marketplace.

Interview 10–15 practice owners about lead generation, consultation conversion, and current software frustrations.

Build a clickable prototype — the questionnaire and a provider-results screen.

Test it on the Howell practice website and measure completion and booking requests.

Build a limited MVP using privacy-by-design principles and legal review.

Recruit 5–10 pilot practices and validate whether they'll pay for measurable results.

Expand geographically only after repeatable acquisition and practice retention are demonstrated.

Central thesis

Help patients find the right provider before they know which treatment to ask for.

Prove that the matching journey generates incremental consultations and paying patients — then sell the proven workflow to practices.
